Phase 1: The Creative Process

Evergreen Masterpiece Guide ↓

Masterpiece Heuristic: To build something that lasts, you must reject short-term hacks. Spend significant time crafting deep utility rather than chasing a quick hype cycle.

  • Time Investment: Real masterpieces aren't built in a weekend. Commit to depth and longevity.
  • Enduring Utility: Solve an acute, structural human or business problem, not a temporary technical gap.
  • Value-First Optimism: Make value to make money. Reject defensive cynicism.
Pure Hype High Utility

Phase 2: Positioning & Packaging

Evergreen Positioning Guide ↓

Positioning Rule: Wrap your work in a single sentence that hits like a hammer. You must know exactly who your product is for and what it does.

  • The One-Sentence Hook: If you cannot explain the value proposition in one direct sentence, you do not understand it.
  • Niche Focus: Win a devoted, passionate core group first. Do not dilute your product trying to appeal to a broad, indifferent market.
  • Simplicity: Over-engineered structures fail. Keep your logic direct and focused on the core promise.
Over-engineered Simple & Direct

Phase 3: Marketing & The Launch

Evergreen Distribution Guide ↓

Distribution Rule: Avoid wasting resources on rented media, transient PR campaigns, or paid hype. Focus on building assets you own and fostering direct user connections.

  • Owned Media Pipelines: Establish direct lines (e.g., newsletters, private communities) to your audience.
  • Word-of-Mouth Potential: Design your product so that the usage itself encourages sharing. An outstanding product is its own best marketing.
  • Long-Tail Consistency: The launch is just one day. Build a system that compoundingly attracts users over years, not weeks.
None (Push Sales) Highly Virally Shareable

Phase 4: Platform & Longevity

Evergreen Platform Guide ↓

Platform Rule: A single product is highly vulnerable. Expand your work into an ecosystem. Create a suite of related utilities and plan on a ten-year horizon.

  • Ecosystem Extensions: Expand your core product with APIs, integrations, or developer toolchains.
  • The Ten-Year Test: Ask yourself: "Will this solve a problem that still exists in a decade?" If not, pivot to a fundamental human need.
  • System Autonomy: Design self-sufficiency and low maintenance overhead so you can focus on building the next asset.
High maintenance grind Autonomous/Passive
